# Election catalog sources

This development snapshot contains 435 regular voting House contests, five separately identified delegate contests, 35 Senate contests, 36 state gubernatorial contests and three territorial gubernatorial contests. All 514 records have named candidate inventories and source links. Exact jurisdiction/seat-set validation is separate from the level of source verification below. A complete independent audit of every official ballot is not claimed.

## Federal candidate inventory and finances

The [DeepSyte designated candidate export](https://deepsyte.app/data/candidates-2026.csv), obtained November 3, 2026, contains 1,294 candidate rows grouped into 440 House/delegate and 35 Senate contests. The normalized federal inventory contains 1,293 candidates after the explicit American Samoa correction below; including governors, the catalog has 1,430 candidates. Credit: **DeepSyte**; each record links its publisher race page. Its [open-data page](https://deepsyte.app/data) expressly offers these exported records for free use with credit. Its [general terms](https://deepsyte.app/terms) restrict service scraping; this build uses the designated export and does not scrape the service or reproduce its proprietary scoring, original analysis or design.

The publisher describes the file as its on-ballot candidate inventory, compiled from state candidate lists and recorded nominees, including some candidates without FEC IDs. It is a publisher compilation; the app does not represent every row as independently matched to a certified original. A candidate's FEC ID and campaign-finance figures do not themselves establish ballot eligibility. Finance remains separate from polling or results. Undated finance, including an undated zero in the export, is shown as unavailable. Dated zero and signed cash balances, including 16 negative cash figures, are retained as reported in the designated export; original FEC forms were not independently audited. The source filing-through date accompanies finance.

District `00` is canonical at-large. CNMI's source delegate district `01` is normalized to `00`, with the original capture preserved. Delegate races never count toward the 435 voting seats. Florida and Ohio Senate contests are typed as specials for Class III seats; the other 33 are regular Class II contests. Each has a distinct seat ID. Term start/end dates remain null where not established.

An independently re-opened [American Samoa official certified midterm list](https://www.aselectionoffice.gov/candidates-information/candidates-for-the-2026-midterm-election) certifies exactly five delegate candidates: Aumua Amata, Fualaau Rosie Tago Lancaster, Meleagi Suitonu Chapman, Sandra King Young and Tuifagaloa Dr. Afu Lefaoseu. The publisher export's extra Samana Semo Veavea is removed only from normalized data, with the raw six-name inventory, the five retained names, reason and official evidence recorded in the race and ledger. The longer publisher name Aumua Amata Coleman Radewagen remains intact with `ballotName: Aumua Amata`; legitimate aliases are not dropped. This targeted primary correction does not imply a full all-state official-ballot audit.

The resulting mechanism check compared all five delegate inventories. DC's official federal sample ballot confirms all three named candidates and corrects Kymone Freeman's publisher `OTHER` designation to **DC Statehood Green** (`G`). CNMI's official listing confirms both candidates, retaining Kimberlyn Kay King-Hinds as the official full-name alias. The VI official sample ballot confirms both names and parties. Guam's two names are corroborated by the explicitly official-derived Vote Clock and Vote Smart Guam ballot pages; GEC's linked Google-hosted original was not opened, so official candidate-set verification remains unclaimed. The raw export contains the American Samoa extra itself; its header has no ballot-eligibility filter, so this was an upstream inventory defect rather than an import-filter misreading.

The bounded Senate-special check finds the Florida general candidate list's three Qualified entries match the imported names. Neil J. Gillespie's official NPA designation is normalized to the independent/non-party display group with raw and normalized values recorded. The linked Ohio official sample-ballot PDF returned 403; its candidate set remains publisher-sourced rather than falsely marked officially checked. Official Senate roster classes establish the seat identities separately from ballot eligibility.

## Governors and original-source links

The [GovGalaxy designated race export](https://govgalaxy.com/data) supplies 71 Senate/governor race records and linked official documents. All 36 state governor candidate pages were retrieved and cached on November 3, 2026; candidates were parsed from their explicit candidate sections. Credit: **GovGalaxy**, “2026 Senate and governor races,” [data page](https://govgalaxy.com/data), accessed November 3, 2026. Its data page calls these sourced public records reusable and asks users to cite the page/file URL and access date.

Candidate party designations are preserved in `partyLabel`; broader D/R/I/L/G/OTHER groups support display without discarding the source designation. Unverified governor incumbency stays explicitly unknown (`incumbencyKnown: false`), rather than inferred from a name or party. No publisher ratings, predictions, AI scores or polling averages are imported.

Official documents linked by a publisher remain identified as links, not claims that this build independently audited every ballot. Some are county sample ballots or prior-stage documents. They must not be treated as authority for every House district merely because they are in the same state; per-race `metadataVerification` preserves the inventory's publisher-compilation status. The administrator-directory link, where present, is explicitly navigation rather than a candidate-list audit.

## Territorial executive tickets

- **CNMI:** all three governor/lieutenant-governor tickets transcribed from the captured [official Commonwealth Election Commission 2026 candidate listing](https://www.votecnmi.gov.mp/candidate/2026-certified-candidates-listing).
- **U.S. Virgin Islands:** all five printed executive tickets transcribed from page 1 of the captured [official November 3 general sample ballot](https://vivote.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2026-General-Election-St.-Croix-District.pdf). The unnamed write-in line is not fabricated as a named candidate.
- **Guam:** two tickets corroborated by the [National Governors Association's 2026 section](https://www.nga.org/governors/elections/) and [Vote Clock's expressly dated GEC sample-ballot transcription](https://voteclock.org/guam-candidates-on-the-ballot-2026). The [official GEC general-election information page](https://gec.guam.gov/2026-general-election-info/) was retrieved using normal Windows certificate validation. Its Google-hosted ballot/list was not opened, so independent official-ballot verification of these two tickets is not claimed. Only factual candidate/ticket names and parties are transcribed; publisher graphics/prose are not reproduced.

Running mates are ticket metadata, not additional governor contests. Territory governor records are excluded from the 36-state denominator and from congressional voting-seat calculations.

## Dates, stages and special elections

Louisiana's House records retain the November 3 open-primary and conditional December 12 next stage, using the [FEC 2026 calendar](https://www.fec.gov/documents/5910/2026pdates.pdf) and [Louisiana stage rules](https://www.sos.la.gov/elections-voting/how-candidates-are-elected). A candidate leading November returns would not thereby become a winner. Dates and current source verification remain explicit.

The [House Clerk vacancy record](https://clerk.house.gov/Members/ViewVacancies) was captured November 3. It lists FL-20 and TX-23 special-election dates as TBD; other listed 2026 vacancies have successors. No November 3 federal House special was verified or invented. Historical special contests are not merged into the current regular contest. This is a bounded reconciliation statement, not a claim about every possible unrecorded event.

## Polling and unavailable live results

Four original-source-checked national generic-ballot observations are supplied separately, never attached to individual district/Senate/governor races. Each links its original release. CBS's 1,572 is weighted N. Pew's field start is explicitly corrected from the API's September 30 to the original's September 28, preserving both values. Ipsos's **unweighted** LV question base of 1,212 is separate from the adult sample of 1,765 and its interval; its original topline's unweighted reduced-base definition is recorded in the sample-basis correction. Emerson's exact leaner convention remains unverified. Original denominator/residual categories remain intact; no compatible national average or win probability is computed.

No live election-result pipeline is established. Every race has `available: false`, an empty vote array and null result-as-of/call/certification. These are missing-data states, not zero vote counts. Media calls and official certification remain separate. No licensed AP/DDHQ feed is copied or proxied.

## Verified Senate carryover

The actual [official Senate roster](https://www.senate.gov/senators/) and [official party-division page](https://www.senate.gov/about/parties-leadership/party-division.shtml) were captured November 3, 2026. The builder parsed all 100 seated roster rows and their state, party and seat class, reconciling 53 Republicans, 45 Democrats and two independents with the second-session party table. It excludes the 33 Class II seats (20 R, 13 D) and the Florida/Ohio Class III special seats, both currently Republican, leaving **32 D, 31 R and two I** carryovers. Exact seat IDs, the two special-seat rows and all 65 carryover rows are preserved in the local ledger. This is a roster/class derivation, not an inference from nominees or an election result. Party and caucus membership are not conflated; the two independents stay separate. Official source timestamps were not supplied, so source-as-of remains null with a separate access date.

## Rebuilding and validation

Run `python build_data.py` beside this file with the preserved source cache available. `--fetch` fills missing designated governor-page captures with at most three concurrent public requests, preserving successful files. The development source cache is outside the published app; `data.json` is the public snapshot. The build validates independent expected House seat and Senate/governor jurisdiction sets, unique candidates within a race, finite/null financial values, all required candidate/source details and honest empty result states. The local canonical ledger records raw-file hashes, exact expected sets and unresolved verification levels.

No Elections in USA ratings or district context was used in this snapshot, so its separately downloaded CC BY-SA file is not redistributed or incorporated. The public data's source-specific notices above apply; no blanket claim that all compilation data is public domain is made.
